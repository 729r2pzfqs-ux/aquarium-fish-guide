About Least Killifish

The least killifish holds the distinction of being one of the smallest vertebrates in the world, with females reaching about one inch and males barely half an inch. Despite its name, it is not a true killifish but a livebearer belonging to the family Poeciliidae. Native to the southeastern United States, from South Carolina to Louisiana, it inhabits shallow, vegetated margins of ponds, swamps, and slow streams. Least killifish are inconspicuous in appearance, with olive to tan bodies and a subtle dark lateral stripe, but their tiny size and peaceful nature make them fascinating subjects for dedicated nano tank enthusiasts. Females give birth to just 2-5 fully formed fry at a time, one of the smallest broods of any livebearer.

Care Tips

Expert Tips

Least killifish can thrive in nano tanks as small as 3-5 gallons with gentle filtration and dense planting. They prefer warm water at 68-80°F and adapt to a wide range of water chemistries. Feed micro-foods including crushed flakes, vinegar eels, micro-worms, and live or frozen baby brine shrimp. Their tiny mouths cannot handle standard-sized foods. Keep them in species-only setups or with other truly tiny, peaceful species, as virtually any common aquarium fish is large enough to eat them. They breed slowly compared to guppies, producing very small broods of 2-5 fry every few weeks. Dense vegetation provides essential cover for fry and adults. They are underappreciated gems for dedicated nano fishkeepers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size tank does a Least Killifish need?

A Least Killifish needs a minimum tank size of 5 gallons (19 litres). They should be kept in schools of at least 8.

What water parameters does a Least Killifish need?

Least Killifish thrive in water between 66-75°F (19-24°C) with a pH of 7.0-8.0.

Least Killifish are considered easy to care for. They are peaceful fish with a lifespan of about 3 years.
